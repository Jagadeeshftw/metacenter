description: The waterfall, bond payout order and reserve, as the deployed pox-5 contract implements them.

Line numbers refer to the deployed pox-5 source, which is identical to stacks-core tag 4.0.1 (commit 62e03cc). A copy is in the repository at `research/pox-5.deployed.clar`.

## Distributions

Once per **distribution interval** (1,050 Bitcoin blocks, two per reward cycle), anyone may call `calculate-rewards`. The sBTC that reached pox-5 since the previous call is the **gross pool** for that interval. The call is booked to the reward cycle that contains its calculation height, which is the last block of the interval.

## The waterfall

<Steps>
<Step>

### Bonds first

Each active bond is owed a **target** for the interval: `shares × target-rate ÷ 10000 ÷ 50` (<Pox5 lines="2266" />). Each bond receives `min(target, what is left)` (<Pox5 lines="2269-2272" />).

</Step>
<Step>

### Reserve: 15% of the remainder

After bonds, 15% of what remains goes to the reserve (<Pox5 lines="2190" />, `RESERVE_RATIO u1500`). If no STX is staked that cycle, the whole remainder goes to the reserve.

</Step>
<Step>

### STX-only stakers: the rest

The other 85% is shared flat per uSTX among STX-only stakers (<Pox5 lines="2191-2201" />).

</Step>
</Steps>

## Bond order

Bonds are paid in **descending `stx-value-ratio`**, with ties going to the **lower bond index**. The contract enforces this: a call with bonds in the wrong order fails with `ERR_INVALID_BOND_PERIOD_ORDERING` (<Pox5 lines="2285-2299" />). Within a bond, rewards are flat per staked sat (<Pox5 lines="2304-2309" />): no staker in a bond is paid before another.

So when the pool is short, the bond paid last in this order is the first to be short-paid.

## The reserve: a back-stop, not an automatic one

The reserve is designed as a back-stop for bonds: capital that keeps bonds whole when mining rewards fall short of their target ([Reserve fund, Stacks docs](https://docs.stacks.co/learn/bitcoin-staking/glossary#reserve-fund)).

In this first iteration it can't be drawn automatically. `transfer-from-reserve` (<Pox5 lines="2696" />) is private and uncalled, and using the reserve goes through a SIP process. An automated process is planned for a later iteration. In a shortfall the reserve deposit is zero, so the reserve stays flat: it does not drain.

<Callout title="Why this matters for the metrics">
Because it cannot be drawn automatically today, Metacenter does not count the reserve as protection already in place. It shows it as **hypothetical cover**: how many cycles of obligation it would equal at today's sizes, if the SIP process made it payable.
</Callout>
